# Omnio in a year

*The long-term product vision. Not a plan — a description of what should be
true, so that we can tell whether we are drifting.*

---

## The feeling

A year from now, Omnio should feel like a **quiet room in your own house**.

Not a tool you visit. Not a service you log into. A place that is already open,
already knows the shape of what you do, and does not perform. You go there to
finish something, and you leave when it is finished.

The emotional target is narrow and unglamorous: **relief**. The small relief of
not having to think about where a file went, whether a website kept a copy of
your passport, whether the PDF you are about to send is the old version, whether
the thing you did last month can be done again without remembering how.

Software that produces relief is rarely exciting on first contact. It becomes
indispensable on the fortieth.

## What using it is like

The experience we are building toward has a particular rhythm.

**You arrive with a file, not a question.** You do not browse a catalogue of
tools or learn a vocabulary. You bring the thing you have, and Omnio meets you
with the two or three things a person in your position would plausibly want. The
right next step is usually already on screen, and choosing it is one movement.

**It has been paying attention, and it says so plainly.** Omnio notices that
this scan has no text in it, that this is the fourth version of the same
contract, that the PDF you exported no longer matches the document it came from.
Every observation carries its reason, because an observation without a reason is
just a machine asserting authority. You can always see why, and you can always
tell it to stop.

**It does not act while you are not looking.** Omnio prepares; you decide. This
is not timidity — it is the difference between an assistant and a process
running on your files.

**It gets easier the longer you use it.** Doing a job twice is enough for Omnio
to offer to remember it. Nobody designs a workflow, draws a diagram, or
configures a pipeline. The work *is* the configuration. A year in, your Omnio
should be noticeably more useful than a stranger's, and you should never have
spent an evening setting it up.

**It is calmer at month twelve than at hour one.** This is the hardest thing on
this page and the one we should be judged on most severely. Most software gets
more cluttered as it accumulates your history. Omnio should get *quieter*: fewer
things asking for attention, better guesses, more that is simply handled. If a
mature workspace ever feels worse than an empty one, we have failed at the thing
that makes Omnio worth building.

## What people say

The sentences we are aiming for are not compliments about the product. They are
statements about the person's own work.

> "I stopped emailing myself files."

> "I didn't realise how much I disliked uploading my documents until I stopped."

> "It told me the PDF was out of date. It was right. I'd have sent the wrong one."

> "I don't really think about it. It's just where I do that."

> "I set it up for my mother and she never called me about it."

And one we should want more than any of them, from someone technical:

> "I checked. Nothing leaves."

The compliment we should be suspicious of is *"it has so many features."* That
is what people say about products they have not yet integrated into their life.

## What problems disappear

If we get this right, several ordinary indignities quietly stop happening.

**The upload tax.** The routine of handing a private document to an unknown
company because you needed one thing done to it, and the low-grade unease
afterwards. That transaction should stop feeling normal.

**Version confusion.** Sending the wrong file. Discovering afterwards that the
attachment was the draft. Omnio sees both the source and what came from it, so
this becomes a thing that gets caught rather than a thing you apologise for.

**The re-learning tax.** Rediscovering, every few months, how you did something
last time. Which settings. Which order. Omnio remembers so that you do not have
to, without asking you to describe what you want in advance.

**The accumulation tax.** The slow silting-up of a workspace until it is easier
to start a new folder than to find anything in the old one. Omnio should notice
what is finished and offer to clear it, so tidiness is something that happens to
you rather than a chore you schedule.

**The account tax.** Signing up, being remembered, being emailed, being
segmented. There is nothing to sign up for and no profile to hold. This does not
disappear because we are generous; it disappears because there is nowhere for it
to live.

## What does not change

These are not features. They are the terms on which the product exists. If any
of them is traded away, what remains is a file converter with good manners.

**Your files stay on your device.** Not "encrypted in transit". Not "deleted
after two hours". Not there at all. Where an exception is genuinely unavoidable,
it is stated at the moment it matters, in the product, in plain words — never in
a policy document nobody reads. One honest exception is worth more than a
hundred reassurances.

**Verifiable, not trustworthy.** Anyone should be able to open the network panel
and watch us keep the promise. We will never ask for trust that cannot be
checked, and we will never make checking embarrassing for us. The day the
evidence contradicts the claim, the claim changes or the behaviour does — never
the wording.

**A file has one understanding.** What Omnio knows about a document does not
depend on which screen you are looking at. Contradiction between screens is not
a rough edge; it is the product being two products.

**It explains itself.** Every observation says why. Every irreversible thing
says so before it happens, not after. Omnio would rather stay silent than
perform certainty it does not have.

**It refuses.** The tool count is not a scoreboard. Anything that does not
strengthen what Omnio is gets removed rather than kept "for completeness" —
including things that took real work to build. A product is defined as much by
what it will not include.

**It works where you are.** Both languages, both directions, keyboard alone,
screen reader, no server, no network. Not as accommodations bolted on for a
subset of users, but as the ordinary condition of the product.

**It is honest about being young.** Alpha says alpha. Nothing claims to be
finished before it is, and no version number flatters us.

## How we will know

Not by usage graphs — we do not collect them, and we should not want them.

We will know by what people ask us. If the questions are "how do I get it to…",
we are still a tool. If they become "can it also notice when…", we have become
part of how they work.

And the strongest signal is the quietest: **someone uses Omnio for a year and
has nothing to say about it**, because nothing about it required their
attention, and their work is simply done.

That is the goal. Not to be impressive. To be unremarkable, in the specific way
that a well-made thing you own becomes unremarkable — while quietly being the
reason a hundred small annoyances stopped happening.
